## Introduction

Pattern language is a term that originated in architecture but has since been applied to several fields, including software design and user experience (UX) design. In UX design, pattern language refers to a systematic collection of established design solutions to common design problems that serves as a reference guide for designers. The use of a pattern language in UX design helps ensure consistency, usability, and efficiency in the design process.

A pattern language for UX design is essentially a library of design patterns that have been established through experience and research. These patterns provide designers with a common vocabulary and a set of best practices for solving common design challenges, saving time and effort by avoiding the need to research and test every design decision from scratch. In addition, the use of a pattern language in UX design leads to a consistent and familiar user experience, leading to greater user satisfaction and efficiency.

## Benefits of a Pattern Language in UX Design

**Consistency**

Consistency is one of the key benefits of using a pattern language in UX design. By relying on established patterns, designers can avoid having to reinvent the wheel for each new design challenge and ensure a consistent experience for users. This consistency leads to greater user satisfaction as users become familiar with the application and are able to complete tasks more efficiently.

> For example, a pattern for creating a login form might specify the placement of the form, the type of inputs required, and the behavior of the form upon submission. By applying this pattern consistently across a website or application, users will be able to recognize the login form and understand how to interact with it, regardless of which page they are on.

**Efficiency**

The use of a pattern language also helps designers to be more efficient in their work. By having a reference guide of established patterns, designers can save time and effort by not having to research and test every design decision from scratch. This allows them to focus on the unique aspects of their design and create solutions tailored to their specific users and requirements.

> For example, a pattern for creating a shopping cart might specify the placement of the cart icon, the behavior of the icon when items are added to the cart, and the visual indication of the number of items in the cart. By using this established pattern, designers can focus on the unique aspects of the shopping experience, such as the layout of the product pages or the checkout process, rather than having to design the cart from scratch.

**Usability**

The use of a pattern language in UX design also leads to greater usability. By providing a consistent experience for users, they are able to more easily complete tasks and navigate the application. In addition, established patterns often incorporate best practices for accessibility, such as clear labeling and accessible color choices, leading to an even more usable experience for all users.

> For example, a pattern for creating a navigation menu might specify the placement of the menu, the behavior of the menu when it is open or closed, and the labeling of the menu items. By using this established pattern, users will know where to find the navigation menu and how to interact with it, leading to a more efficient and enjoyable experience.

## Steps to create a Pattern Language

Creating a pattern language for UX design can seem like a daunting task, but with a systematic approach, it can be a valuable tool for designers. A pattern language is a collection of established design solutions to common design problems, organized into a cohesive system that serves as a reference guide for designers. The use of a pattern language helps ensure consistency, usability, and efficiency in UX design. This article outlines the steps for creating a pattern language for UX design.

**Step 1: Conduct a Needs Assessment**

The first step in creating a pattern language is to conduct a needs assessment. This involves identifying the common design problems faced by designers and documenting the design solutions that have been found to work well. Designers can start by reviewing previous design projects, conducting user research, and reviewing industry literature. This information can be used to identify common design problems, such as navigation, forms, or error messages.

**Step 2: Document Design Solutions**

Once the common design problems have been identified, the next step is to document the design solutions that have been found to work well. This may involve creating sketches, wireframes, or prototypes of the design solutions. It is important to document not just the visual design, but also the behavior and functionality of each solution. This documentation should include details such as the placement of elements, the type of inputs required, and the behavior of the design upon submission.

**Step 3: Organize the Patterns into a Cohesive System**

With the design solutions documented, the next step is to organize them into a cohesive system. This may involve grouping the patterns by category, such as navigation, forms, or error messages. Each pattern should be clearly defined and documented, with guidelines for implementation and examples for reference.

**Step 4: Define the Pattern Language**

The next step is to define the pattern language. This involves creating a clear and concise definition of each pattern, including its purpose, goals, and benefits. The definition should also include guidelines for implementation and examples for reference. The pattern language should be organized in a logical and easy-to-use format, such as a table or flowchart, and should be accessible to all designers.

**Step 5: Implement the Pattern Language**

With the pattern language defined, the next step is to implement it. This involves using the pattern language to guide the design of new projects, ensuring consistency and usability. Designers should also provide feedback on the pattern language, noting any areas that need to be refined or updated.

**Step 6: Review and Update the Pattern Language**

Maintaining the relevance and currency of a pattern language is one of the challenges in its creation. As technology and design trends evolve, patterns may become outdated or new patterns may emerge. To address this, designers should regularly review and update their pattern language to ensure its ongoing usefulness.

## Common UX Design Patterns (Section from Techuz.com)

### 1. Input and output

(Calendar picker, a popular input design pattern for selecting dates. Source: Airbnb)

Users need to enter the information in several places on the interface. It can be for signing up, choosing a date for booking, entering contact and other personal details, credit card numbers, etc. The input and output patterns are aimed at making the data input easy and at the same time providing them feedback about the entered information. Examples of input and output patterns are as follows:

*   **Password strength meter:** This helps users to create a strong password to prevent malicious password cracking.
*   **Calendar picker:** The calendar picker allows users to choose the date to enter, sort or track data. The calendar graphics within the interface make it easy to choose the date or date range and make quick decisions.
*   **Good defaults:** Good defaults prefills certain user data that are likely to match. It is useful when the system can make the appropriate guesses of the input fields. For example, input fields country or location.
*   **Captcha:** Captcha helps reduce spamming by differentiating human users from automated bots.
*   **Preview information and updates:** Previewing information helps users double-check that the inputs are correct and accurate as they need. It helps them safely explore the interface resulting in better interaction and experience.
*   **Wizard:** Wizard breaks down one big task into small sub-tasks and helps users complete it step by step and one at a time. It is highly useful when the user has to input complex and large amounts of data and follow specific steps in sequence.

### 2. Navigation

(Navigation tabs and breadcrumb — patterns that make navigation easy. Source: Adidas.com)

To perform tasks and get the information, the user has to locate features and browse through the product. Navigation patterns are aimed at making this easy by reducing friction and increasing learnability. They help users understand where they are now and how to reach to the point they want. Common examples of navigational UX patterns include:

*   **Navigation and module tabs:** Tabs divide a large amount of data into separated sections, making them more accessible and easy to navigate. It is the most common navigation pattern that you’ll find across all digital products.
*   **Pull-to-refresh gesture:** Pull-to-refresh gesture on certain devices help users load new content with a simple pull-down gesture. It’s the standard pattern to refresh the content in social media, chat, email news and content apps.
*   **Breadcrumbs:** Breadcrumbs works as a visual aid helping users know their location in the site’s hierarchy. This makes it easier for them to go back and forth and thus simplifies the navigation.
*   **Progressive disclosure:** To help users focus and easily navigate, you need to minimize the clutter and cognitive load. Progressive disclosure helps you do that by presenting only the required data and displaying other details upon request.

In conclusion, a pattern language is a valuable tool for UX designers, offering a reference guide of established design solutions and ensuring consistency, usability, and efficiency in UX design. By creating and maintaining a pattern language, designers can focus on the unique aspects of their design, create better experiences for their users, and advance the field of UX design.
