A [button](https://uxcel.com/glossary/buttons) is only as good as its label. Users decide whether to click based on a few words, and vague or confusing text creates hesitation. The label carries almost all the meaning. It tells users what will happen, sets expectations, and builds enough trust to take action. Get it wrong and even a perfectly designed button fails. [Icons](https://uxcel.com/glossary/icons) can support [labels](https://uxcel.com/glossary/labels), but they rarely replace them.

Placement, alignment, [contrast](https://uxcel.com/glossary/contrast), and formatting all play a role in whether labels get read or skipped. Writing effective button labels isn't about being clever. It's about being clear, concise, and specific enough that users never have to guess what they're about to do.

## Place icons on the left

![Image 5: Place icons on the left Best Practice](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69aae3f2b94d73d122ba807e_a-1696972137205-2x.jpeg)

![Image 6: Do](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/699eddabcee97eb5db208fcd_good.png)

![Image 7: Place icons on the left Bad Practice](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69aae3f2b94d73d122ba8087_b-1696972137214-2x.jpeg)

![Image 8: Don't](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69f22e6504b99af0df9d88bb_bad.png)

An icon can give users a hint about the button's purpose. Leading icons are commonly placed before the label in left-to-right languages. [Research](https://uxcel.com/glossary/research-knowledge) shows that in left-to-right languages, users scan [pages](https://uxcel.com/glossary/pages), paintings, movie screens, and just about everything from the left.[[1]](https://app.uxcel.com/courses/ui-components-n-patterns/button-label-best-practices-673#references) For this reason, placing an icon on the left helps identify a button and its purpose much faster. When it appears after a button label, the icon serves only as decoration.

Additionally, placing your icons on the left will leave room on the right for a downward pointing arrow when you need a [dropdown](https://uxcel.com/glossary/dropdown)[menu](https://uxcel.com/glossary/menus).

If icons are self-explanatory and recognizable, users won't need to read the label. Use a leading icon to grab their attention.

> **Pro Tip!** Place the icon after the label for directional actions (like “Next →”) or trailing indicators (like checkmarks or spinners)

## Use meaningful button commands

![Image 9: Use meaningful button commands Best Practice](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69aae3f2b94d73d122ba808e_a-1696972183700-2x.jpeg)

![Image 10: Do](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/699eddabcee97eb5db208fcd_good.png)

![Image 11: Use meaningful button commands Bad Practice](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69aae3f2b94d73d122ba8098_b-1696972183701-2x.jpeg)

![Image 12: Don't](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69f22e6504b99af0df9d88bb_bad.png)

Button label [commands](https://uxcel.com/glossary/commands) must be crystal clear, especially for actions that can’t be undone. Users should immediately understand what will happen when they click a button.

Rather than using words like “yes” or “no” in your buttons, use clear action verbs like “delete” or “cancel.” This leaves no doubt in the user’s mind what action they’re completing.

## Apply recognizable icons

![Image 13: Apply recognizable icons Best Practice](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69aae3f2b94d73d122ba8091_a-1696972275586-2x.jpeg)

![Image 14: Do](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/699eddabcee97eb5db208fcd_good.png)

![Image 15: Apply recognizable icons Bad Practice](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69aae3f2b94d73d122ba8094_b-1696972275587-2x.jpeg)

![Image 16: Don't](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69f22e6504b99af0df9d88bb_bad.png)

Icons are simplified [images](https://uxcel.com/glossary/images) showing a button's purpose. They can make the function of a button immediately recognizable to users, without needing to read the accompanying label.

Universal icons — those that are instantly recognizable and used across digital products — are generally the best choice for common tasks such as returning to the home page or sending an [email](https://uxcel.com/glossary/email). Their immediate recognizability speeds up the user journey and requires less thought.

## Accompany icons with labels for more clarity

![Image 17: Accompany icons with labels for more clarity Best Practice](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69aae3f2b94d73d122ba8084_a-1696972371319-2x.jpeg)

![Image 18: Do](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/699eddabcee97eb5db208fcd_good.png)

![Image 19: Accompany icons with labels for more clarity Bad Practice](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69aae3f2b94d73d122ba8081_b-1696972371319-2x.jpeg)

![Image 20: Don't](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69f22e6504b99af0df9d88bb_bad.png)

Icons are great for saving space and adding a visual element to your design, but they can sometimes leave users guessing about their function. That's why pairing them with text labels is often a smart move.

Labels clarify the purpose of an icon, reducing ambiguity and enhancing [user experience](https://uxcel.com/glossary/user-experience). Think about it: an icon of a floppy disk might not mean "save" to a younger audience. Labels make sure everyone's on the same page. Plus, adding text next to an icon boosts [accessibility](https://uxcel.com/glossary/accessibility). Screen readers can articulate the label, offering a more inclusive experience for users with visual impairments.[[2]](https://app.uxcel.com/courses/ui-components-n-patterns/button-label-best-practices-673#references)

![Image 21: Maintain color consistency Best Practice](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69aae3f2b94d73d122ba80a0_a-1696972513937-2x.jpeg)

![Image 22: Do](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/699eddabcee97eb5db208fcd_good.png)

![Image 23: Maintain color consistency Bad Practice](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69aae3f2b94d73d122ba80a3_b-1696972513938-2x.jpeg)

![Image 24: Don't](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69f22e6504b99af0df9d88bb_bad.png)

While establishing the [visual hierarchy](https://uxcel.com/glossary/hierarchy) of your buttons, you may come up with multiple [color](https://uxcel.com/glossary/color) ideas. Remember, however, that the same color should be applied to button labels and icons for better scannability and [legibility](https://uxcel.com/glossary/legibility). Take time to find the right colors, and don't hesitate to seek feedback from users or colleagues.

## Use contrasting label color

![Image 25: Use contrasting label color Best Practice](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69aae3f5b94d73d122ba8107_a-1696972769187-2x.jpeg)

![Image 26: Do](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/699eddabcee97eb5db208fcd_good.png)

![Image 27: Use contrasting label color Bad Practice](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69aae3f5b94d73d122ba8120_b-1696972769188-2x.jpeg)

![Image 28: Don't](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69f22e6504b99af0df9d88bb_bad.png)

Selecting the right color for your button labels is more than just an aesthetic choice; it's also about accessibility and user experience. High contrast between the text and its background is crucial. It helps ensure that the text is readable, not just for users with perfect vision but also for those with visual impairments. The [Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG)](https://uxcel.com/glossary/wcag) recommend a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text.[[3]](https://app.uxcel.com/courses/ui-components-n-patterns/button-label-best-practices-673#references)

## Fit a label into a single line

![Image 29: Fit a label into a single line Best Practice](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69aae3f5b94d73d122ba8118_a-1696972806182-2x.jpeg)

![Image 30: Do](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/699eddabcee97eb5db208fcd_good.png)

![Image 31: Fit a label into a single line Bad Practice](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69aae3f5b94d73d122ba811d_b-1696972806182-2x.jpeg)

![Image 32: Don't](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69f22e6504b99af0df9d88bb_bad.png)

Breaking a button label into two lines usually creates confusion for users, making them more likely to choose the wrong action. To maintain user-friendly design, it's advisable to fit your button labels into a single line whenever possible.

However, exceptions do exist. For instance, some specialized [call-to-action](https://uxcel.com/glossary/call-to-action) buttons may have secondary text situated above or below the main label. If you decide to take this approach, always remember to conduct A/B tests to gauge the button's effectiveness. Importantly, the primary action the button serves should remain clear and confined to a single line for the sake of readability.[[4]](https://app.uxcel.com/courses/ui-components-n-patterns/button-label-best-practices-673#references)

## Center a label within a button

![Image 33: Center a label within a button Best Practice](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69aae3f5b94d73d122ba8126_a-1696972839435-2x.jpeg)

![Image 34: Do](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/699eddabcee97eb5db208fcd_good.png)

![Image 35: Center a label within a button Bad Practice](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69aae3f5b94d73d122ba8115_b-1755674083794-2x.jpeg)

![Image 36: Don't](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69f22e6504b99af0df9d88bb_bad.png)

Label alignment within buttons is a crucial detail that can significantly influence user [interaction](https://uxcel.com/glossary/interaction). By centering the label both horizontally and vertically within the button, you create a balanced visual focal point. This symmetry not only enhances the button's aesthetic appeal but also makes it easier for users to identify and interact with the button quickly.

> **Pro Tip!** If your button contains a label and an icon, align them as though they are one element.

## Simplify label style

![Image 37: Simplify label style Best Practice](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69aae3f5b94d73d122ba812a_a-1696972914538-2x.jpeg)

![Image 38: Do](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/699eddabcee97eb5db208fcd_good.png)

![Image 39: Simplify label style Bad Practice](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69aae3f5b94d73d122ba8123_b-1696972914539-2x.jpeg)

![Image 40: Don't](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69f22e6504b99af0df9d88bb_bad.png)

When it comes to button labels, less is often more. Italics, underlining, and other additional formatting should be avoided. Even punctuation marks like exclamation points or periods at the end of a label should be skipped, as they can create visual clutter.

Instead, consider using a heavier text weight than your [body copy](https://uxcel.com/glossary/body-text) to make the label stand out. The goal is to make the label's message as clear and straightforward as possible, minimizing the chance of confusing users. Keep the styling simple and the message clear, and you'll create buttons that users find easy to interact with.

## Cut off unnecessary words

![Image 41: Cut off unnecessary words Best Practice](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69aae3f5b94d73d122ba810c_a-1696972881912-2x.jpeg)

![Image 42: Do](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/699eddabcee97eb5db208fcd_good.png)

![Image 43: Cut off unnecessary words Bad Practice](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69aae3f5b94d73d122ba812d_b-1696972881912-2x.jpeg)

![Image 44: Don't](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/61f9082050036c6c4b4899f8/69f22e6504b99af0df9d88bb_bad.png)

When crafting [microcopy](https://uxcel.com/glossary/ux-microcopy) for buttons, clarity and brevity go a long way. Users typically scan rather than read, and UI elements often come with space limitations. This means every word on your button should be essential.

Here are some go-to tips for choosing the right words:

*   **Use verbs:**Words like "Buy," "Submit," or "Download" make it clear what action will occur.
*   **Be specific:** Instead of vague labels like "Go" or "Send," use "[Search](https://uxcel.com/glossary/search)" or "Subscribe" to provide more context.
*   **Keep it short:**Limit the label to two to three words to ensure it's scannable and fits well within the button.

> **Pro Tip!** It’s okay to ignore some grammar rules — like leaving out articles or prepositions — in favor of better UX as long as the meaning is still clear.
