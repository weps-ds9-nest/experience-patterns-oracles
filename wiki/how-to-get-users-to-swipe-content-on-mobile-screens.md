# How To Get Users To Swipe Content On Mobile Screens

## Making visual cues visible

One of the most effective techniques for displaying content on mobile screens is to use bleeding edges. With a small and limited screen, you have to use all the space you got.

Bleeding edges allow content to bleed through the screen edges to maximize space. When users see this visual cue, it signifies more content to be revealed. However, users may miss it and fail to swipe for more content if you don’t design it correctly.

To ensure users see the cue, bleed at least half of a content piece. Make sure to bleed enough to where users can touch it with their finger and swipe.

Avoid bleeding too little content, or users won’t notice that it’s swipable. The amount of content to bleed should be about a finger width. This size allows the finger to hit the touch target comfortably.

## Progress Indicators

Suppose users miss the bleeding content cue because they’re too focused on the center of the screen. Page indicators act as a backup cue that can also signify swiping. They indicate the number of scroll views contained in a row.

However, if your indicators are too small, users could easily miss them. Many designers use little dot indicators, but they’re not always easy to see. A better approach is to use small segments because they have more width.

The active segment has the high contrast fill, while the inactive ones have lower contrast. As users swipe, the fill will move to the next segment. Content should also bleed through the left and right edges. These cues signify that they can swipe left or right to see the next or previous view.

## Arrow Buttons

You shouldn’t always rely on swiping as the only way to reveal more content. Some users don’t have the manual dexterity to do so easily. Providing arrow buttons can help those users browse content via tapping.
