We can agree that having to reset your pin after inputting it for the umpteenth time can be very frustrating. Sometimes our memory fails us or we don't just remember previously complicated passwords. We can also agree that the process of resetting your pin can be a bit tasking. However, I believe that providing instructions that are clear, concise, useful, and conversational can make a difference. In this article, I will be sharing my process of rewriting the reset pin screens to improve the user experience.

### Project overview

Dodopay is a fintech platform aimed at providing better payment solutions for people in Africa. The app serves as a wallet and a platform where you can make financial transactions on the go. Financial transactions include bill payments, international & local transfers, savings, loans, and mobile money transfers.

### Timeline

The project took me about one week to complete. With research, I was able to discover the best processes and tone I could use for a ‘reset pin’ screen.

### My Role

I was the UX Writer solely responsible for coming up with the content used in the ‘reset pin’ process. I was tasked with the responsibility to research and come up with the best practices for an improved user experience.

### The Process

**Understanding the ‘reset pin’ process:** I wanted to make the process of resetting passwords for our users easier and stress-free. Dodopay uses the ‘secret word’ method for security. I noticed that most people didn't remember their secret words and it posed an issue because it brought the user to a dead-end thereby, causing more frustration for the user. Sometimes, new customers forget the account altogether which is not good for the business. The aim at all times is to create a great user experience that leaves the user coming back…again and again.

**The research:** Many users care less about resetting their password unless it’s that important. Users might eventually decide to reset their password because of how necessary it is. It is pertinent to make the process of resetting passwords a seamless one. **What makes a good reset process:**

*   It shouldn’t take your customer more than a minute to reset their password
*   Asking security questions or asking for secret words can provide a negative experience for the user. Most people don’t even remember their secret words. Verification code or OTP is an easier and simpler way for top-tier security.
*   There must be a link that directs users to reset passwords to ease their stress.
*   Allow the user to generate their password themselves.
*   Place holders help to guide users on what to type in each box.

I put all these points into consideration while rewriting the screens and process. I also aimed to be clear, concise, and helpful in my instructions so that the user is carried along in all processes.

**Drafts and content Ideas:** While writing out the content ideas, I put in mind the brand’s voice and tone which is confident, understanding, and friendly. The brand voice & tone coupled with the basic UX Writing principles (clear, concise, useful, and conversational) informed my decision on the best content for each screen.

**Testing:** I tested the desired content on users to get feedback to see how I can further improve the content. Using A/B testing on 5 users, the new iteration won by 4 to 1. The new iteration had an easier process and they were able to finish in seconds. At a point where users were asked to enter their phone number in the original screen, I noticed that users who put in 2349055455554 instead of 23409055455554 would face more difficulty. We all know that the best way to write a number with the country code is to omit the first zero. Hence, clear placeholders telling the customers what to type in is very important.

**Final Correction**: From the testing, I made a few iterations to come up with the final content. I put in clear placeholders where necessary and clear instructions to guide the customer through the whole process.

**Tools**: I used **Miro** to create the design and content for each screen.

### What I learned

Building a good password reset process is more than asking security questions. If your password reset process makes life harder for your customers, you’ll be giving them a reason to stop using your service.
