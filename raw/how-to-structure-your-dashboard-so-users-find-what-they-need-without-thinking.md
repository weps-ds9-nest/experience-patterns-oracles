Ever walked into a well-organized grocery store? You know exactly where to find the milk, bread, and produce without asking for help. The layout just makes sense. Now imagine walking into a store where everything’s randomly placed. Frustrating, right?

Your dashboard works the same way. Users come looking for specific information, and how you organize it determines whether they leave satisfied or confused.

Let’s explore the proven ways to structure dashboard information that actually work with how people think and behave.

## 1. The Newspaper Principle

Newspapers figured out information hierarchy 150 years ago, and their approach still works perfectly for dashboards.

**Headlines grab attention first:** The most important news gets the biggest, boldest treatment at the top of the page.

**Sub-headlines provide context**: Supporting details help readers understand the bigger picture.

**Body text offers depth:** Full stories are available for people who want complete information.

**Everything has a clear purpose:** Each element serves a specific role in the information hierarchy.

## Applying This to Your Dashboard

**Level 1: The Headlines**: Your 2–3 most critical metrics, large and prominent at the top. These answer “What’s the most important thing happening right now?”

**Level 2: The Supporting Cast:** Key performance indicators and trend indicators that provide context for your headlines.

**Level 3: The Details**: Breakdowns, filters, and detailed charts available for users who need to investigate further.

## Real Example in Action

Instead of showing 15 equal-sized charts, organize like this:

**Headline**: “Revenue: $2.3M (+12% vs target)”- Big, bold, immediately visible 

**Supporting**: Monthly trend chart, top performing products, regional breakdown 

**Details**: Drill-down capabilities, filters by time period, detailed product performance
