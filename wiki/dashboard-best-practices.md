# 9 Best Practices When Designing a Dashboard

Effective dashboard design requires nine key practices ensuring users can quickly find information and make decisions. Understand user needs through research, decide end goals, pick dashboard type, use five-second rule, go minimalistic (cognitive psychology shows humans can't process vast information simultaneously), emphasize information architecture with hierarchy, choose KPIs carefully based on audience research, provide context and comparison values, and prioritize aesthetics alongside functionality. Books judged by covers; dashboards judged by appearance—both matter equally.

## Key Patterns & Concepts

- **Understand User Needs**: Perform research; learn what users want/need; determine most important data to use
- **Decide End Goals**: Establish end goals; determine what dashboard presents; avoid simply "going with gut"
- **Pick Dashboard Type**: Choose from operational (measure/monitor/manage short-term), strategic (long-term strategies), tactical (mid-management growth formulation), analytical (comprehensive data for executive insights)
- **Five-Second Rule**: Users willing to spend 5 seconds finding information; dashboard must provide this
- **Go Minimalistic**: Avoid cramming multiple visualizations; cognitive psychology shows brain can't process vast information simultaneously; limited scope works best
- **Emphasize Architecture**: Data hierarchy and layout critical; design needs balance emphasizing important elements over less important
- **Choose KPIs Carefully**: Successful dashboard = KPI dashboard; use audience research selecting best KPIs to use
- **Provide Context**: Numbers carry no meaning without context; provide comparison values; add titles to all charts; give maximum information in provided space
- **Prioritize Aesthetics**: Treat dashboard like publisher would design book—book judged by cover, dashboard judged by appearance; pick right color combination, format, font, layout after data and hierarchy complete
- **Low App Retention**: When dashboard doesn't provide five-second information access, may cause low retention problems
- **Cognitive Load**: Brain limited scope for information processing; minimize information; balance multiple visualizations

### Nine Best Practices

**1. Understand the Needs of the User**
Data collected through user research seriously impacts design quality and UX. Perform research learning target audience. Determine what users want/need. Based on this information, determine most important data for dashboard.

**2. Decide on an End Goal**
Can't go with gut. Success demands having multiple end goals in place. Among end goals, one is deciding what dashboard presents.

**3. Pick a Dashboard Type**
Four main types to choose from: Operational (measure, monitor, manage operations with immediate/shorter timescale for BI purposes), Strategic (long-term business strategies analyzing essential information based on trends), Tactical (array of data helping formulate mid-management growth strategies), Analytical (large comprehensive data used by analysts creating insights helping business growth at executive level).

**4. Use Five-Second Rule**
Dashboards give users access to relevant information. Main purpose: resolve frequently asked questions within short timeframe. Use five-second rule—user willing to spend five seconds finding needed information. Does dashboard provide? If not, may be reason for low retention problems.

**5. Go Minimalistic**
Don't go overboard with dashboard. Multiple visualizations crammed with too much information. According to cognitive psychology, brain can't process vast information simultaneously. Scope limited—minimalism best option.

**6. Put Emphasis on Information Architecture**
Everything from data hierarchy to layout important for success. Design needs balance—aim for design emphasizing most important elements over less important.

**7. Choose KPIs Carefully**
Successful dashboard = KPI dashboard design. KPIs are metrics displaying visual representation of most relevant data based on different business/brand areas. Use audience research selecting best KPIs.

**8. Provide Context**
How are users supposed to get message without context and comparison values? Numbers on dashboard carry no meaning without context. Users won't know if action required. When designing, provide maximum information in provided space. Even if data seems obvious, may appear strange to user. Provide comparison values and add titles to all charts.

**9. Don't Forget About Aesthetics**
Live in society focused on aesthetics. Designer must treat dashboard like publisher would design book. Books judged by cover; dashboards judged by appearance. Finish data and hierarchy, then move onto dashboard aesthetics. Pick right color combination, format, font, and layout.
