## Start with a Clear Vision

It is important to have the image of your dashboard before you start creating one. You have to think of the structure, the colors, the navigation even creating grouping categories and filtering. You should have in mind that people from other departments might see your dashboard and it have to be easy for them to understand what you want to say with your charts.

### 1. Know your audience and purpose

First you need a purpose. Different users have different goals. A marketing team might need insights into campaign performance, while the finance team focuses on cost management. So, you should focus first on questions like: What you want your dashboard to talk about? Meaning what questions you want your dashboard to answer? Which is your audience?

Defining the purpose of your dashboard first hand, will help you focus on the right metrics avoiding unnecessary complexity.

### 2. Keep it simple

A cluttered dashboard can overwhelm users. Also, distracts them from the important metrics that your dashboard would have. Focus on what matters most to keep it clear and concise. A minimalistic dashboard is easier to read. So, don’t overcroud it with too many charts or text.

### 3. Highlight key information

Most users read a dashboard from left upper corner to the right. So its essential to place the most ctitical KPIs of your analysis there. Use large, bold, numbers to make them stand out.

### 4. Create a Consistent and Intuitive Design

Stick to a consistent color palette to avoid confusion. Use the same colors for the same data categories across charts. If you are not sure about the colors that you should use, you can check color theory first. It will help you to improve your knowledge about the combinations of colors that you will be use for your dashboard. Don’t use too many colors, stick with two or three across your entire dashboard to have a uniform palette.

### 5. Make it Interactive

Interactive elements helps users to have control over the data that are most interested in and not only see one aspect of your data. Interactive visualizations can also reaveal patterns and trends that might not be visible at first sight.

## Example Dashboard

In the example dashboard below I will explain thoughly all the above steps that I wrote about. You can find the dataset that I used [here.](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/jessemostipak/hotel-booking-demand)

I have a dataset that has information about bookings of two hotel types. My main goal here, is answering questions in my bashboard like:

1.   How well each type of hotel performs, for instance are there many cancellations compared to the bookings that they have? Or from which country most bookings come from?
2.   I would also, like to have a comparison between the two hotel types to see how different the customers behave in those.
3.   Moreover, I would like to know the demographic differences of those hotel types.

**Plan your dashboard layout**

_Before reading further, if you are not familiar with dashboard architecture, have a look at it first and then try to make your own._

Sketch your dashboard layout now! Use tools like Canva to quickly visualize your design. I always make a rough sketch on paper with some ideas and then I use canva.com to create a wireframe. There are many website that you can do that, make a quick research to see which fits best for your needs.

Before starting building your dashboard take some time to answer the questions below:

1.   Which navigation and filters am I going to use?
2.   Am I going to use any grouping in the dataset?
3.   Which charts would fit in my representation? (You can read few articles about how to pick the right chart for your purpose)
4.   How am I going to label my charts?
5.   Which colors and fond am I going to use?

If you are done with the above questions, then I am sure, you are ready to make a great dashboard! Play with different styles and colors to find your style. Enjoy!
