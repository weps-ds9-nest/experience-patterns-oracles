# 3 Patterns for Trustworthy AI-Enabled Customer Support

Trust is not just a feeling—it's a trait organizations exhibit through how they provide service and can be designed for. While AI chatbots now feel remarkably human and can be adaptive, contextual, and empathetic, blind trust in AI systems is dangerous. Transparent, accountable, and controllable solutions are essential, especially when support involves sensitive needs or distressed users.

## Key Patterns & Concepts

- **Show Capabilities and Limitations**: Help users calibrate trust before and during service use; explaining what systems can/cannot do improves understanding and trustworthiness
- **Offer Task-Level Suggestions**: Improve perception of AI through demonstrated competence; people trust systems more when they remain in control
- **Adapt to Content and Context**: Make services accessible and inclusive; timely support for those who need it most improves outcomes drastically
- **Transparency and Control**: Design so users remain in driving seat; avoid sacrificing transparency for efficiency
- **Responsibility and Accountability**: Hide seams in services carefully; don't hide responsibility mechanisms behind "efficiency"
- **Extreme Cases Need Human Touch**: Some situations require capable human intervention—design with fallback paths to human support
- **Avoid Dangerous Blind Trust**: AI chatbots create illusion of competence; without mechanisms of responsibility and accountability, people give too much trust

## Full Article

When designing for trust, distinguish between trust (a feeling given by people to organizations) and trustworthiness (a trait organizations exhibit through service). Trustworthiness can be designed for.

### The Problem: People Want Human Support But Bad Support Breaks Trust

"I just want to speak to someone" is common in services, especially when people are distressed or have urgent needs requiring care. Unfortunately, most systems use first-come-first-served approaches that don't consider urgency. Even when humans are involved, bad processes can fail users—being passed from agent to agent without understanding, or being sent to someone who can't help.

### AI Chatbots Feel More Human Than Ever

Large Language Models enable chatbots that think and write like people. For customer services drowning in ticket backlogs, this is great news. Recent studies show 50% of consumers think AI agents can be empathetic when addressing concerns.

For customer services, these advances mean AI solutions can be adaptive to contexts, tailor language for dignity and respect, learn and self-optimize without complete re-development, and augment human services while ensuring people remain more in control.

### Blind Trust in AI Chatbots Is Dangerous

Intelligent systems present illusions of competence and hide seams in services. When responsibility, transparency, or accountability mechanisms are hidden, people give too much trust to services that don't deserve it—often at their peril.

Recent studies reveal harms: Stanford research found AI therapy chatbots contributed to harmful stigma and dangerous responses. People in relationships with AI partners on ChatGPT saw relationships ruined by model updates. An emergent "AI-associated psychosis" shows deification of AI as god-like super-intelligence leading to psychosis.

### Three Design Patterns for Trustworthy AI Customer Support

**Pattern 1: Show Capabilities and Limitations**

Help users calibrate trust before and while using service. Explaining what intelligent systems can and cannot do improves understanding and trustworthiness. This is critical before expecting users to rely on AI support.

**Pattern 2: Offer Task-Level Suggestions**

Improve perception of AI through demonstrated competence. People trust intelligent systems more when they remain in the driving seat, maintaining control and agency.

**Pattern 3: Adapt to Content and Context**

Make services accessible and inclusive. Timely support for those who need it most drastically improves outcomes. Sensitive situations benefit from AI that understands context and adjusts approach.

### Transparency and Control Cannot Be Optional

Regardless of underlying technology, organizations must provide transparent, accountable, and controllable solutions that empower users. Risks are real and consequences significant.

Perhaps extreme cases will always need capable human touch. In the meantime, AI-enabled support agents will continue improving, and design patterns are key to creating responsible AI-enabled services that serve people.
