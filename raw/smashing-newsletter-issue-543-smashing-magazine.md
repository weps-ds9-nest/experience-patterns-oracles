### 1. Bulk Upload

Many bulk upload features are lacking, so how can we do better? Drawing on her experience from improving bulk upload in an **online recruiting tool**, Livinda Christy shares [solutions to common issues and challenges](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/ux-case-study-bulk-upload-feature-785803089328). She walks you step by step through the process of creating a hassle-free file import and also offers tips for defending your design decisions in front of stakeholders.

### 3. Icon Labels

When you use icons in an interface, do you usually add text labels? If not, you might want to reconsider your approach. As Christopher Butler points out in his post “[In Defense of Text Labels](https://www.chrbutler.com/in-defense-of-text-labels),” even the simplest icons can create ambiguity, **slowing down users’ orientation** within an interface. By combining text labels and icons, you create clearer affordances than either element could on its own.

[![Image 4: In Defense Of Text Labels](https://mcusercontent.com/16b832d9ad4b28edf261f34df/images/3bd79f5d-ba42-b372-7a3a-127b1a4fab83.png)](https://www.chrbutler.com/in-defense-of-text-labels "In Defense Of Text Labels")
### 4. Top Vs. Side Navigation

Top navigation or side navigation — which is the best choice for your context? Taras Bakusevych [compares both](https://uxdesign.cc/top-navigation-vs-side-navigation-wich-one-is-better-24aa5d835643) to find out **where each of them performs best**. As he concludes, top navigation uses little space and works well when there aren’t too many navigation items, whereas side navigation is a good fit for products with a large number of navigation links, as it is easily scalable and configurable.

[![Image 5: Top Vs. Side Navigation](https://mcusercontent.com/16b832d9ad4b28edf261f34df/images/fd004be1-b934-5d18-cece-6c364b3baa8c.png)](https://uxdesign.cc/top-navigation-vs-side-navigation-wich-one-is-better-24aa5d835643 "Top Vs. Side Navigation")

### 6. Toast Messages UX

Toast messages gently remind users about a successful event or change of state, **without interrupting them** from their main task. However, there are also some things we need to watch out for when designing toasts and snackbars. Vitaly summarized what you need to know to avoid common usability issues and [make your toast messages effective](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/vitalyfriedman_design-guidelines-for-toast-messages-ux-activity-7235182514091274240-qwfp/).

### 8. Modal Vs. Page

Let’s say you’re designing a registration flow, and one of the steps requires some more information. Do you add an additional page or use a modal overlay? Ryan Neufeld synthesized **guidelines**, heuristics, and recommendations into a [flowchart](https://uxplanet.org/modal-vs-page-a-decision-making-framework-34453e911129) that helps you assess which component best suits your project’s needs.
