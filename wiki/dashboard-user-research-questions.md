# 30 User Research Questions for Dashboard Design

Effective dashboard design requires understanding four areas: user goals, context of use, timeliness and interactivity requirements, and user familiarity with data and metrics. Without proper user research, dashboards risk becoming fancy-looking charts that aren't informative or actionable. Here are 30 research questions designed to uncover real user needs.

## Key Patterns & Concepts

- **User Goals**: Understand strategic (tracking progress), operational (monitoring real-time), and analytical (identifying opportunities) dashboard purposes
- **Context of Use**: Discover when users access dashboards, how often, for how long, and what triggers data reviews
- **Decision and Action Triggers**: Identify what data prompts immediate action and what thresholds require intervention
- **Timeliness Needs**: Separate real-time vs. periodic data requirements; understand what needs trends vs. single points in time
- **Interactivity Requirements**: Determine what comparisons users make and how they share information downstream
- **Data Expertise Levels**: Assess user sophistication with data analysis, business metrics, and industry terminologies
- **Data Literacy**: Understand how familiar users are with tools, metrics, and potential confusion points
- **Metric Selection**: Learn how users currently track metrics and awareness of peer practices

## Full Article

Deciding relevant information for dashboards is tricky. When you ask users what they want to see, they typically request every metric ever produced, plus more.

In dashboard design, it's not enough to ask what users want. You must understand why these data are needed and how they would be used. Without proper user research, dashboards risk creating fancy-looking charts and graphs that aren't informative or actionable.

There are three main dashboard purposes: Strategic (setting goals, tracking progress), Operational (monitoring real-time performance), and Analytical (identifying opportunities, investigating problems). Users often have multiple purposes—production managers monitor productivity while performing root cause analysis and tracking objectives.

### Research Area 1: User Goals

**General goal questions:**
- What are the top goals and priorities in your role?
- How do these numbers help you in your role/achieving certain goals?
- What are the most important tasks you or others need to perform using analytics?
- Why is having specific data or metrics important?

**Strategic dashboard questions:**
- What does success look like to you?
- How do you measure your success?
- Imagine evaluating your progress today—how would you do it?

**Operational dashboard questions:**
- What decisions do you frequently need to make?
- What questions do you regularly ask yourself or your team about the business?
- Walk me through a normal day at work, from arrival to departure.

**Analytical dashboard questions:**
- How do you identify opportunities for improvement?
- Tell me about the last time you used data to understand a problem.
- What mistakes or missed opportunities can be prevented with data?

### Research Area 2: Context of Use

Context of use helps prioritize data points. Designing for a quick glance differs from designing for deep analysis.

**General context questions:**
- Tell me about the last time you used metrics.
- What triggers you to look into data?
- In what context would you review the dashboard?
- How often do you review metrics?
- How much time do you usually spend reviewing numbers?

**Outcome questions (decisions & actions):**
- What would you do if you had certain data?
- How do you use these numbers to make a decision?
- What data would prompt you to immediate action? What are the thresholds?
- If you see this number increase/drop/plateau, what would you do next?

### Research Area 3: Timeliness and Interactivity

Data can be expensive. Separating data requiring real-time vs. periodic updates is beneficial. This information often emerges from context-of-use questions but can be probed further.

**Timeliness questions:**
- What information do you need on a daily basis?
- How often do you review specific data or metrics?
- What information do you need to see in trends vs. a single point in time? Why?

**Interactivity questions:**
- What do you compare these numbers to? (historical, average, company, industry, location)
- Once you see this data, what would you do next?
- How do you share the information with others?

### Research Area 4: Familiarity with Data, Metrics, and Terminologies

Users fall anywhere along the spectrum from complete data illiteracy to statistical expertise. Understanding user sophistication helps create usable dashboards.

**Data analysis familiarity:**
- Tell me about the last time you worked with data. What tool did you use? What questions were you trying to answer?
- What do you often use Excel for?
- Show me a report or analytics tool you enjoy using. Why? (Do users prefer in-depth data or quick answers?)

**Business metrics and terminology familiarity:**
- What metrics do you currently track? Why are they important?
- How do you decide which metrics to track?
- Are you aware of other metrics your peers might use?

**Industry and analytics terminologies:**
- What challenges did you face when first using analytics in your job?
- (Observation) Note user's language and terminology understanding
