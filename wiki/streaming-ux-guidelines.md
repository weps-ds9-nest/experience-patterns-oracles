# 6 UX Guidelines for Video Streaming Platforms

Streaming platforms must prioritize user workflows over content discovery chaos. Six core guidelines improve streaming UX: quick access to continue-watching content, easy removal of unwanted history, explained recommendations, automatic intro/outro skipping with manual override, smart recap skipping for binge viewers, and dark backgrounds reducing eye strain. While content quality drives platform adoption, thoughtful UX improvements impact brand perception and user satisfaction.

## Key Patterns & Concepts

- **Continue Watching First**: Surface continue-watching section immediately after hero banner; minimize clicks before users reach in-progress content
- **Content Removal**: Enable easy removal of accidentally-watched or unwanted items; skip recording if watched <few minutes or thumbs-downed
- **Recommendation Transparency**: Show "Because you watched..." or "You might like this because..." rationale for recommendations
- **Auto-Skip Intros**: Automatically skip repeated opening sequences during binge-watching; show skip button for unique/new season openers
- **Auto-Skip Outros**: Auto-skip credits with countdown; but alert viewers to post-credit scenes (Marvel trend, Game of Thrones extras)
- **Smart Recap Skipping**: Skip recaps for binge-watchers watching multiple episodes back-to-back; show recaps for weekly releases or viewing session starts
- **Dark Backgrounds**: Use black and gray backgrounds—88% of TV viewers watch evenings in darkness; bright backgrounds strain eyes (avoid Paramount+ bright blue)
- **Binge-Watch UX**: Streamline sequential episode play; prioritize momentum over content discovery

## Full Article

Over years of using many streaming apps, I've observed that some platforms lack good design and UX, yet remain used because of content. While content drives adoption, UX improvements impact brand perception. Here are 6 design patterns that should be standard across streaming services.

### Guideline 1: Quick Ways to Continue Watching

Large part of streaming is binge-watching—viewing multiple episodes back-to-back. Yet with many platforms, it takes several clicks or scrolls to reach "Continue Watching" section. While highlighting new content is important, immediately after that should be where you jump back into in-progress content.

Netflix shows hero banner, then rows of "East Asian Crime," "Trending," "Popular"—before "Continue Watching" appears way below fold. HBO Max gets it right: after hero banner, "Continue Watching" appears first, requiring minimal effort.

### Guideline 2: Remove Content I'm No Longer Interested In

Friends use my account watching shows I dislike. I watch few episodes of new shows before dropping them. I accidentally click wrong content. All contaminate "Continue Watching" section.

Always provide way to easily remove items. Surprising how uncommon this is. Browser options exist on some platforms; app support nearly non-existent.

Also, if only played few seconds/minutes, it was likely mistake—don't show under "Continue Watching." If thumbs-downed, probably won't continue—ask if user wants removal (unless hate-watching).

### Guideline 3: Explain Recommendations

Recommendations are great—help navigate large catalogs. Platforms should explain why recommending something. Obvious when credits roll: "You may also like." Less obvious on welcome screen. HBO Max recommending _Pennyworth_ with no context. Netflix showing "East Asian Crime" section—why?

Add "You might like because..." or "Because you watched..." widgets when recommending. Netflix showing entire East Asian Crime section should explain or let users say "No thanks, not interested."

### Guideline 4: Skip Intros and Outros

**Intros**: Most episodes start with opening sequence—usually same every episode. Binge-watching, you don't need to see intro every time. Jump to episode. While popular services now show "Skip Intro" button, advocate for automatic skip.

Exception: Intro is unique/new (new season start). Show "Skip Intro" button. Also when intro begins minutes into episode, show "Skip Intro" button.

**Outros**: Offer skip option. Given binge-watching, relatively safe to auto-skip outros with countdown to next episode. But extra content appears during/after credits—Marvel post-credit scenes, Game of Thrones behind-the-scenes and sneak peeks.

When extra content after credits exists, give viewers chance to see it and make them aware. Hold off auto-skip and show "Next Episode" button until after scene. Or show "Skip Credits," "Watch Behind the Scenes," "Watch Next Week Preview" button. Goal: alert user of additional content and provide skip option.

### Guideline 5: Skip Recaps for Binge Watchers

Recaps ("Previously on...") are useful if haven't watched episode in week+. But binge-watchers watching episodes back-to-back need no recap—they're locked in, ready for action.

Skip recaps for binge-watching episodes back-to-back. Show recaps for weekly releases or first episode of viewing session.

### Guideline 6: Don't Use Bright Background Colors

Colors used on splash screens, profile selection, and home screens matter. 88% of TV viewers prefer evenings (busiest 9pm). Usually dark outside. Even if not, people dim lights and close blinds for optimal TV viewing.

Streaming apps should use colors easy on eyes at night. Netflix and HBO Max use black/gray backgrounds. Paramount+ uses bright blue—strains eyes until reaching content.
