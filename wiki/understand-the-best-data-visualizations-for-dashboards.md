# Understand The Best Data Visualizations For Dashboards

## “The effectiveness of data visualization can be gauged by its simplicity, relevancy and its ability to hold the users hand during their data discovery journey” — Jagat Saikia

Photo by Milad Fakurian on Unsplash

**What is a Data Visualization Dashboard?**

A [data visualization dashboard](https://www.sigmacomputing.com/blog/how-to-build-data-visualization-dashboards/#:~:text=What%20is%20a%20data%20visualization,to%20make%20more%20informed%20decisions.) is an interactive application for tracking, analyzing, and displaying Key Performance Indicators (KPI), metrics, and data points. Technical and non-technical individuals can utilize these dashboards to comprehend company intelligence and make better decisions. Users can benefit from the data produced by data dashboards by using it to identify performance metrics and better understand patterns and occurrences during the analysis process. It further helps to generate reports for clients that demonstrates the work completed.

_Source:_[_Building Data Visualization Dashboards_](https://www.sigmacomputing.com/blog/how-to-build-data-visualization-dashboards/#:~:text=What%20is%20a%20data%20visualization,to%20make%20more%20informed%20decisions.)_by Rachel Serpa_

**How to design a Data Visualization Dashboard?**

A data dashboard should have an easy-to-understand design so that you can quickly comprehend the information displayed rather than spending hours trying to figure it out. Excellent dashboards are simple, dynamic, and easy to use. Prioritizing the most relevant data, considering usability, and following primary business objectives should form the cornerstone of an effective dashboard design UX.

**A Pre-attentive Dashboard**

Data dashboards include a variety of visual representations. Some of these visual representations are simple to comprehend, even before we investigate further, we begin to make sense of them. These are called [preattentive features](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dashboards-preattentive/) and it is a major element to take into account while designing dashboards.

Illustration by DrawKit

The human brain can quickly recognize the length of some objects and their location in 2D space, but it takes more effort to comprehend and estimate area, color, and angle. It is vital to take these elements into account when selecting a data representation option for the dashboard.

Here are a few examples of poor data visualizations.

**Pie Charts**

[Pie charts](https://bernardmarr.com/why-you-shouldnt-use-pie-charts-in-your-dashboards-and-performance-reports/#:~:text=From%20a%20design%20point%20of,data%20more%20complicated%20than%20before.) visualize numerical data as slices of a circle. This indicates that the numerical data is displayed as an area, angle and arc length of the outside circumference. However, humans are only capable of estimating the arc length of the outside circle but not so good at estimating the angle or area. Furthermore, straight lines are simpler to understand than curved ones, especially when they do not begin from the same point. Pie charts are hence not very useful when comparing figures. Most dashboards have more than one pie chart, which makes comprehending the data even tougher.

Illustration by freepik

Source: [_Why You Shouldn’t Use Pie Charts In Your Dashboards And Performance Reports_](https://bernardmarr.com/why-you-shouldnt-use-pie-charts-in-your-dashboards-and-performance-reports/#:~:text=From%20a%20design%20point%20of,data%20more%20complicated%20than%20before.)_by Bernard Marr_

**When to use a pie chart?**

*   When there are only a few slices
*   When accurate comparisons are not required
*   The primary point is that one object is significantly larger than the other.

**Tree Maps**

Area is the basis for [tree maps](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/treemaps/#:~:text=Comparisons%20Are%20Difficult&text=Treemaps%20rely%20on%20area%20(and,for%20tasks%20involving%20precise%20comparisons.), which visualizes numbers as rectangle-sized shapes. They are frequently employed to display huge data sets made up of thousands or even millions of items and users may feel overwhelmed by this volume of information. Bar charts can be used to replace tree maps when the data consists of one quantitative variable and one categorical variable, while scatter plot graphs can be used to replace tree maps when the data consists of two quantitative variables.

**Stacked Bar Charts**

A stacked bar chart can be considered as an expanded version of a standard bar chart. A stacked bar chart, as the name implies, lays one variable on top of another. Stacked bar charts may appear straightforward, but as the number of variables rises, [they become more complicated to read](https://insightsoftware.com/blog/comparing-data-visualizations-bar-vs-stacked-icons-vs-shapes-and-line-vs-area/).

Illustration by freepik

Then what is the best data visualization technique?

**Bar Charts**

It is recommended to use a bar chart rather than a pie chart or a tree map when statistics need to be compared. People often understand how to read and comprehend information communicated by the graph because the bar chart is a widely used data visualization tool in books, newspapers, and many other contexts.

Illustration by freepik

Source: [_The Advantages of Bar Graphs_](https://sciencing.com/advantages-bar-graphs-6822794.html)_by Rachelle Dragani_

**Bullet Charts**

When you want to indicate whether or not a metric is on track, use a bullet chart instead of a gauge. When several metrics need to be displayed, a bullet chart is the perfect tool for data visualization.

Illustration by freepik

**Conclusion**

Visualizations are an efficient and useful tool for extracting information from data. They serve numerous purposes at various phases of the analysis process. Probably the first thing that comes to mind when you think of data visualization is a straightforward bar graph or pie chart. While these may be a necessary component of data visualization and a standard foundation for many data graphics, the appropriate visualization must be combined with the appropriate set of data. Therefore it is important to create dashboards that are both appealing and simple to understand. This helps people to comprehend it more frequently and to understand more complex items
