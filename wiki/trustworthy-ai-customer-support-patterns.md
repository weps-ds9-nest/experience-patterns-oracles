# Trustworthy AI Customer Support: 3 Design Patterns

AI chatbots enabled by Large Language Models providing quantum leap in capabilities and accessibility when "thinking" and writing like people. However, blind trust in AI chatbots dangerous—intelligent systems presenting illusion of competence hiding seams in services sacrificing transparency for efficiency. When mechanisms of responsibility, transparency, accountability hidden, people give too much trust to services not deserving it often at peril. Particularly dangerous in supporting chatbots delivering support of any kind: Stanford study found AI therapy chatbots contributed harmful stigma and dangerous responses. People entering committed relationships with AI partners seeing relationships ruined following model updates. Emergent "AI-associated psychosis" where deification of AI chatbots leads to psychosis. Three design patterns for trustworthy AI customer support: Pattern 1 "Show capabilities and limitations" helping users calibrate trust before and while using service; explaining what intelligent systems can cannot do improves understanding trustworthiness. Pattern 2 "Offer task-level suggestions" improving perception of AI through competence; people trust intelligent systems more when remain in driving seat. Pattern 3 "Adapt to content and context" making services more accessible and inclusive; timely support for those needing most drastically improves outcomes. Transparency and control cannot be optional—organizations must provide transparent, accountable, controllable solutions empowering users addressing profound new challenges carefully.

## Key Patterns & Concepts

- **AI Illusion Problem**: Intelligent systems present illusion of competence hiding service seams sacrificing transparency
- **Trust Calibration**: Users need understanding system capabilities limitations before engagement
- **Hidden Responsibility**: When accountability mechanisms hidden people give excessive trust undeserved systems
- **Dangerous Trust**: AI therapy chatbots can contribute harmful stigma dangerous responses
- **Show Capabilities and Limitations**: Help users calibrate trust; explain what system can cannot do
- **Capability Transparency**: Improves understanding and trustworthiness through explicit communication
- **Task-Level Suggestions**: Improve perception through competence demonstrations
- **User Control**: People trust systems more when remaining in driving seat making decisions
- **Adapt Content and Context**: Make services accessible and inclusive
- **Timely Support**: Support for those needing most drastically improves outcomes
- **Transparency Imperative**: Transparency and control cannot be optional design considerations
- **Accountable Solutions**: Provide transparent, accountable, controllable solutions empowering users
- **Human Touch Still Matters**: Extreme cases needing capable human resolution requiring human involvement
- **Design Responsibility**: Principles key in creating responsible AI-enabled services serving people
