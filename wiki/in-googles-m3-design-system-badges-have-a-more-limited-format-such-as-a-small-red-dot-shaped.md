In Google’s M3 design system, “badges” have a more limited format, such **as a small red dot-shaped container with/ without numbers.**

Google’s M3 system shows the simplest example of “badges”

So badges could have more reduced features, **even without text** indicating status.

## I thought that was all..

But soon, I was confused again. Google’s Material Design guide didn't have tags, **only “chips.”**

What are chips then?

Source: https://m3.material.io/components/badges/overview

There are four types of chips:

Source: https://m3.material.io/components/badges/overview

1.   Assist: represent an action (assist chip)?
2.   Filter: represent filter results
3.   Input: content is generated by the user
4.   Suggest: content is generated by the product

I could understand that Chips are more interactive, **with selected and unselected statuses,** and directly related to filter functions.

Now, back to our project, our UIs indicated **filter** categories, but they were still not interactive. Does that mean our design decision was wrong?

Then, I found this article on LinkedIn. Luckily, I wasn’t the only one who was confused about the **Chips, Badges, and Tags.**

[https://www.linkedin.com/posts/adhamdannaway_designsystem-design-ux-activity-7097222903376613377-EplX?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/adhamdannaway_designsystem-design-ux-activity-7097222903376613377-EplX?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop)

According to him, I could clarify my confusion.

### Badges are more “flat indicators without any interactions.”

### On the contrary, tags and chips are both interactive, leading to future user actions, such as closing the tap, selecting or deselecting, or clicking through the link.

So, we can say tags = chips; it is simply the way we prefer to call them. The names differ depending on the companies and brands.

In our case, we used these **“chips”** to assist users and inform them of available filter categories.

After delving into Google’s design system, I realized that we should have considered making it a variant that represents both selected and unselected statuses.

In conclusion, because our core intention was to inform users about the filter category to help them(assist) make decisions and learn the categorical system of our app (filter), we can finally call our UI components “ **Chips”!**
