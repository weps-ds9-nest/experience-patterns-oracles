## How to design resilient dashboard experiences that support first-time users, returning users, and edge cases like errors states.

Created by Zeba Dalal

Dashboards are the crown jewel of a product where data comes to life and decisions are made. But behind every polished chart and stat lies a deeper design challenge: **how do you craft a dashboard experience that works not just _when things are perfect_, but also when there’s _no data_, _incomplete data_, or _something breaks_?**

In my work, I approach dashboards as living, evolving interfaces with **three critical UX states:**

1.   **First-Time Experience:** When users see the dashboard for the very first time, and there’s little or no data to show.
2.   **Returning Experience:** When users come back to view meaningful insights, trends, and take action.
3.   **Error & Edge Cases:** When something goes wrong, an API fails, data hasn’t populated, or charts are empty.

Designing for all three states isn’t optional, it’s **essential**. Because real-world usage rarely sticks to the happy path. The best dashboards are those that guide, adapt, and respond to users across every scenario.

## 1. First-Time Experience (No Data State)

This is the most neglected state in design, yet it’s a make-or-break moment for activation.

Created by Zeba Dalal

**What I focus on:**

*   Clear guidance (“Here’s what you can do next”)
*   Smart placeholders and visual cues
*   Friendly, non-intimidating copy
*   Progress bars, tooltips, or even a short onboarding carousel
*   Optional demo data or mock previews

> **“You never get a second chance to make a first impression. 
> 
>  That includes your dashboard.”**
