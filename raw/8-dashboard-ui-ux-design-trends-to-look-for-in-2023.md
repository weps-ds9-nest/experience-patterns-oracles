Dashboard UI/UX design is an essential component of many web and mobile applications. It is the core backend that supports all applications so making it user friendly will also help the mobile and web applications. Dashboard UI/UX design in 2023 will prioritize simplicity, personalization, and user control, while also incorporating the latest data visualization techniques and emerging technologies like AI and machine learning.

### Older Dashboard Designs

Older dashboard designs tended to be more complicated and cluttered, with a lot of data crammed onto a single page. In order to help users grasp the data, they frequently used text-heavy tables and charts with minimal visual hierarchy or organisation. There were typically only a few buttons or links for navigation, and there were frequently little to no choices for modification or personalization.

Old dashboards sometimes lacked branding or visual design, and their aesthetics tended to be utilitarian and functional. The palette of colours was frequently constrained to a few simple possibilities, and the entire design prioritised functionality over beauty.

In general, the emphasis on user experience and user-centric design that we see in modern dashboard design was absent from older dashboards.

## 2023 Trends in Dashboard Design

### 1. Increasing use of data visualisation:

As our ability to collect and analyse data continues to increase, designers will need to come up with innovative ways to visualise data. Dashboard designs will likely feature more interactive maps, graphs, and charts.

1.   Data visualisation can assist users in quickly spotting trends, patterns, and outliers in the data.
2.   **Data is made more clear and accessible by visualisation**, regardless of the level of technical expertise of the audience.
3.   User engagement is increased because to interactive and dynamic data visualisations.
4.   **Data visualisation can assist in identifying flaws or inaccuracies in data**, allowing users to rectify and enhance the data’s quality.
5.   **Data visualisation may help make data more open and accessible to all stakeholders**, encouraging more accountability and transparency.

### 2. Use of minimalism and whitespace:

Minimalism and whitespace will remain popular in dashboard design since they can contribute to the creation of a clear and well-organized user interface.

1.   **White space and minimalism can help to produce a clear, straightforward design** that is simple to comprehend and utilise.
2.   Designers can emphasise important information and make it stand out more effectively by using whitespace to separate pieces visually.
3.   By eliminating visual noise and distraction, minimalism and whitespace can aid to increase readability.

### 3. Personalised Dashboard:

Dashboards that are specifically tailored to each user’s tastes and interests are becoming more and more common thanks to AI and machine learning.

1.   **Designers can build a more specialised and pertinent user experience** by letting consumers customise their dashboard. Users can select how and where they want to view the information that is most relevant to them.
2.   **Customized dashboards can aid in enhancing user engagement by enticing visitors to check the dashboard frequently**. Users are more likely to feel invested in the data and the insights it delivers by offering a more personalised experience.
3.   **By removing extraneous information and emphasising the most crucial insights for the user**, personalization can help to increase the relevance of the data.
4.   **Customized dashboards can improve teamwork** by letting users share their personalised dashboards with colleagues.
5.   **Designers may assist users in making better, more informed decisions based on the data by giving consumers** a customizable dashboard that is suited to their needs.

### 4. Increased user control:

In 2023, dashboards that give consumers more freedom over the data they view, how it’s arranged, and how it’s presented will gain popularity.

1.   These widgets let users change the size and placement of widgets on the dashboard as well as add, remove, and rearrange them.
2.   **Give customers with a variety of filtering choices** so they can quickly focus on particular data elements and narrow down their data.
3.   Allows users to easily alter the layout of the dashboard by allowing them to drag and drop widgets, charts, and other visualisations onto it.
4.   **Options for exporting and sharing customised dashboard views** with others make it simpler for users to work together and share information.
5.   **Provide customers the ability to alter the dashboard’s colour scheme, text size, and other design components** to create a view that is tailored to their tastes.

### 5. Greater use of micro-interactions:

By giving users feedback and direction as they engage with the interface, micro-interactions can make dashboard interfaces more engaging and intuitive.

1.   **Hover effects:** A tooltip or other contextual information appears when a user hovers over a chart or data point, providing more information about the data.
2.   **Animations** can be used to draw attention to data changes or to display the outcomes of user activities with the dashboard.
3.   **Feedback and alerts:** Users can receive notifications or alerts when specific thresholds or conditions are fulfilled.
4.   **Contextual menus:** Contextual menus offer consumers more choices or details when they interact with particular dashboard items.
5.   **Progress bars:** Within the dashboard, progress bars can be used to display the state of data updates, visualisations, or other activities.

### 6. Design for mobile first:

Design professionals will need to produce user interfaces that function well on smaller screens and slower connections as more consumers visit dashboards on their mobile devices.

1.   With limited screen real estate, it’s critical to focus the most crucial indicators and KPIs.
2.   Navigating on a smaller screen might be difficult, therefore it’s critical to **streamline the navigation menu** and provide a distinct hierarchy of information.
3.   Employ responsive design to guarantee that the content and dashboard layout adjust to the size of the screen, delivering a consistent user experience across various devices.
4.   **Reduce the use of images, videos, and other data-intensive features to speed up page times** because mobile customers may have limited bandwidth and poor internet connections.

### 7. Focus on accessibility:

As accessibility becomes more prominent, designers will need to make dashboards that are inclusive of all users and accessible to them regardless of their ability.

1.   To help people who are blind or visually impaired understand the content, include alternative text with all photos and charts.
2.   **Employ high contrast colours**: To make it simpler for people who are blind to read the content, use high contrast colours for both the backdrop and the text.
3.   Make sure the keyboard can be used to access all interactive areas.
4.   **Publish closed captions** or transcripts for videos
5.   **Use illustrative link text**: Because screen readers are used by people who are blind or visually challenged

### 8. Gamification:

By adding gamification components, dashboards can be made more interesting and enjoyable to use while also offering incentives for users to interact with the interface and reach their objectives.

1.   **Progress bars and badges can be used to show users how they are doing in relation to a particular objective** or accomplishment, inspiring them to keep going by giving them a sense of accomplishment.
2.   **To motivate users to do particular tasks** or activities and to add a sense of fun and excitement to the dashboard, challenges and quests can be added.
3.   **The dashboard can be enhanced with personalization capabilities** to provide users the ability to tailor their experience and foster a sense of pride and investment in the dashboard.
