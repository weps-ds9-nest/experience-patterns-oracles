Imagine: You're staring at your screen, and instead of a dull message saying _'Error: No data found_,' you see a sheep floating into a UFO beam, captioned: _'Aliens took your files. We're on it!'._ Suddenly, you're smiling instead of sighing. Weirdly, you don't feel frustrated anymore. Why? Because in the age of boring digital interfaces, a little humor goes a long way.

Empty states don't have to be tedious or frustrating. In a world with digital interfaces everywhere, humor can turn errors into smiles and confusion into delight. Let's explore witty empty states that can make users laugh, not groan, and why that matters. And yes, I will also explain how to create one.

## Why do funny empty states matter?

**1 — Alleviating the pain** is an entertaining empty state's first and foremost value. Ask yourself, 'When do users see these screens?'. The answer is when something terrible happens or the user is at the cold starting point in an experience like posting, finding friends, progressing, etc. Therefore, users feel they need more support and guidance. Whatever, it points to a pain. For example, in posting experiences on social media, the pain is the friction of entering a new journey while concerned about:

*   How should I start?
*   What should I write about?
*   Who will see my post?
*   How do they feel about it?
*   What kind of content is appreciated more here?

An entertaining empty state, which is inevitable, can turn their feelings into better ones. Following this approach, users will not look at a white paper. However, they are using a blank mind in this case, and it doesn't still feel good enough. Providing some info or ideas can have a lot to say.

**2—The** second value is **incentivizing people to take the desired action**. When people encounter a page that is not what they expected or tells them something unpleasant, such as losing their saved content, the page will ask them to take one specific action. This action may involve trying again, returning and checking out later, waiting, approving something, following someone, etc.
