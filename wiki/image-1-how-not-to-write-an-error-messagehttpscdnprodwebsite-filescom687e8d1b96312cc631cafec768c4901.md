Error messages always mean well. But all too often, they’re confusing, unclear, and leave you wondering what to do next. In this post, we’ll highlight some of the worst error messages we’ve ever seen — and offer tips on how to make sure your errors never make these big mistakes.

_Big tip o' the hat to the amazing_[_Erika Hall_](https://twitter.com/mulegirl)_for sparking the conversation I pulled these examples from._

## Blame the user, obviously

One of my favorite characteristics of digital interfaces has got to be _snark_. Like, I just _love it_ when an app has opinions on my cleverness, appearance, background, or ability to use interfaces.

![Image 3: White text over red background reads: Your Tweet was over 140 characters. You'll have to be more clever. ](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/687e8d1b96312cc631cafec7/68c490181202aaaa3643d236_60108264b92ae24f103d9231_6002086f72b727080001d952_twitter-error-message.jpeg)

‍H/t to[@angelakilduff](https://twitter.com/angelakilduff/status/976155684990431232)(with sympathies for her having to deal with this apparently invulnerable message)

All reports suggest that Twitter has obliviated this highly offensive bit of microcopy “wit,” so don’t go flame them.

Not for this, anyway.

### Suggested revision for this error message

_Tweets must be under 140 characters, including spaces._

Doesn’t accuse me of lacking cleverness _and_ it offers me a hint about something tangible I can do to tighten things up — namely, get rid of spaces.

## Call me names, like ineligible or invalid

![Image 4: Text reads: You're ineligible for that request. There may be a problem with either the data or your eligibility. Continue browsing the site.](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/687e8d1b96312cc631cafec7/68c490181202aaaa3643d242_601082635ce191745501ec7a_6002086f72b7274d7601d8b6_unhelpful-error-message.jpeg)

‍H/t to[@thischrisoliver](https://twitter.com/thischrisoliver/status/976205404978991105)

Ummmm … pretty sure I _am_ eligible to make that request, as I did indeed just make it.

Perhaps — and I’m just fishing here — you should have deftly guided me away from making said request, instead?

Also, identifying two possible causes of the error rather undercuts the big red assertion above. Whenever possible, narrow the cause down to one reality, and speak specifically to that.

### Suggested revision for this error message

Without knowing what exactly the user was trying to do here, it’s hard to suggest something different, but a generic revision could start with:

_Sorry, due to your permission levels or a mistake on our part, you can’t access that._

## Be delightfully nonspecific

![Image 5: Large white popup with small text that reads: "error" in the center and a button that says "OK" on the bottom right.](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/687e8d1b96312cc631cafec7/68c490181202aaaa3643d23c_60108264b4ba454027843938_6002086f72b727364901d7b3_nonspecific-error-message.jpeg)

‍H/t to[@epersonae](https://twitter.com/epersonae/status/976345306102054912)

While this error certainly has brevity on its side, it’s not what you’d call … useful. Which is a key characteristic of a good error message.

Without specific details on what happened _nor_ even a hint on what to do about it, I’m left in the dark, battling the yawning void of ennui that only the metaphysically inert “OK” button can adequately express.

![Image 6: A large white box. In the center is a small, white exclamation point inside a black triangle.](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/687e8d1b96312cc631cafec7/68c490181202aaaa3643d239_601082646d6bf4446451b0a4_6002086f72b72717ae01d954_google-doc-error-message.png)

‍Google Docs making a missing image rather alarming!

## Give “too real” a chance

![Image 7: Text reads: There is no way to recover from this.](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/687e8d1b96312cc631cafec7/68c490181202aaaa3643d23f_60108264d58c7f905ac2c51c_6002086f72b7273b6201d718_vague-pronoun-error-message.jpeg)

‍H/t to[@TenShookMars](https://twitter.com/TenShookMars/status/976124494963240960)

While I’m a _huge_ fan of writing that remains beautifully meaningful with or without context, it’s not the **best** recipe for an error message.

Nonspecific pronouns like _this_ are all-too-open to misinterpretation — especially of the “this is my life rn” variety.

Anytime you’re writing for the web — be it an error message or a blog post — take a close look at every instance of vague pronouns like _it_, _this_, and _that_ and ask yourself if the whole experience wouldn’t be clearer if you swapped in _the thing_ in place of _that_.

## Cancel my cancellation, I’d not like to not do that

![Image 9: A cacel button next to text that reads "cancel."](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/687e8d1b96312cc631cafec7/68c490181202aaaa3643d25c_6010826371dfb13a29a758ee_6002086f72b72765ce01d750_cancel-cancellation-error-message.jpeg)

‍H/t to[@abunchofrandom](https://twitter.com/abunchofrandom/status/976321635048304640)

Speaking of being specific with your people, places, and things: I present to you the classic case of cancelling your cancellation.

It’s an all-too-common scenario in software product UIs. Person acts to cancel something, then decides they actually didn’t want to cancel after all — meaning that both the desired action _and_ the undo action can be described with the same word: cancel.

### Suggested tactics for a revision of this error message

Thankfully, you’ve got some options:

1.   **Add specifics**: For the desired action, say _what_ you’ll be cancelling. E.g., “Cancel this meeting” or “Cancel your order.”
2.   **Use a cancel synonym**: If your voice, tone, and audience allow for it, you can use a cancel alternative like “Never mind” for the undo action.
3.   **Use affirmative and/or negative language**: Depending on how you phrase the rest of the modal, you can use “yes” and “no” in your confirm and undo actions, respectively. E.g., “Yes, cancel the meeting” and “No, keep the meeting.”

## Just fail

![Image 10: Popup alert reads: Download failed: failed.](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/687e8d1b96312cc631cafec7/68c490191202aaaa3643d275_60108264d5032e42b7ab6102_6002086f72b72749f601d677_slack-error-message.jpeg)

‍H/t to[@AndrewSchmidtFC](https://twitter.com/AndrewSchmidtFC/status/976281500076335107)for courageously volunteering an error from the platform he works on, Slack.

![Image 11: A popup that reads: Something's not right. ](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/687e8d1b96312cc631cafec7/68c490191202aaaa3643d27e_60108264520a6b6cb03f0fda_6002086f72b7276ca101d787_somethings-not-right-error-message.jpeg)

‍H/t to[@designinginward](https://twitter.com/designinginward/status/976474093259317248)

![Image 12: Login popup with email and passwords fields filled out. An orage alert next to the email field reads: Sorry your request could not be completed.](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/687e8d1b96312cc631cafec7/68c490191202aaaa3643d281_601082646d6bf46e1451b0a5_6002086f72b7271c1101d8ff_pinterest-error-message.jpeg)

‍H/t to[@Pamela_Drouin](https://twitter.com/Pamela_Drouin/status/976169555172065281)

Every product designer, dev, and content strategist has run into the unfortunate case where something … just went wrong. And it’s really hard to pinpoint any single cause.

### Suggested tactics for a revision of this error message

If you write for product, whatever your role might be, I’d encourage you to push back for more details in a situation like this. Depending on the answer, you might be able to come up with a more useful generalization — such as, “Hmm, it looks like you might not be connected to the internet.”

Even if you can’t generalize, it’s possible there’s a viable solution that will cover many of the unknown causes for the error, such as refreshing the page, clearing your cache, or troubleshooting your internet connection.

And if you can’t usefully generalize, it might well be time to show a little humility.

The example from Pinterest above shows promise by starting off on the right foot: Sorry! But it’s kinda downhill from there, as typing a username or email into a form field doesn’t exactly equal a “request” from the user perspective.

Always make sure you’re speaking the user’s language — especially when it comes to a simple interaction like logging in. It’s the small things that can _really_ break a user experience.

## Don’t run it by QA

![Image 13: Adobe popup alert reads: Cannot save X for reason Y.](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/687e8d1b96312cc631cafec7/68c490191202aaaa3643d272_60108264d58c7f4acac2c526_6002086f72b727e2b501d6b3_adobe-error-message.png)

‍H/t to[@pbausch](https://twitter.com/pbausch/status/976121980075175936)

Looking at this lovely modal — ironically spotted in one of Adobe’s few tools specifically for writers — I can’t help but imagine the following scenario:

_Engineer: Hey, Writer, what’s our standard for error messages?_

_Writer: Well, we explain what went wrong, and why. What’s your specific error?_

_Engineer: Failed save._

_Writer: Okay, then basically, “Cannot save X for reason Y.”_

_Engineer: Got it. Shipped._

_Writer: Cool! Thanks for checking._

Ah, office miscommunications that make their merry way into prod.

### Suggested tactics for a revision of this error message

In this imaginary scenario, the writer was two-thirds right (if neglectful as far as explaining their variables goes). A good error message does explain what went wrong and why (when possible) — but a key third piece is to explain _how to fix it_. In some cases, the why will make the fix self-explanatory, but not always.

## Why narrowcast when you can broadcast?

![Image 14: Error popup reads: ERROR for site owner: Invalid domain for site key.](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/687e8d1b96312cc631cafec7/68c490191202aaaa3643d278_6010826448b2e37734134408_6002086f72b72777d401d686_google-error-message.png)

There’s absolutely nothing like broadcasting a message meant for the few to _everyone_. That’s what email’s for, Google.

## On the lighter side: if you’re going to mock something, mock yourself

![Image 15: A popup alert over a picture of a dog that reads: No faces could be found in this photo. The two buttom options are, "CANCEL FILTER" or "TRY HARDER."](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/687e8d1b96312cc631cafec7/68c490191202aaaa3643d27b_60108264b4ba45225e843939_6002086f72b727e41601d872_self-mockery-error-message.jpeg)

‍H/t to[@katiehuangux](https://twitter.com/katiehuangux/status/976155360988823552)

Since we started off with horrible user-shaming, it’s only fitting to round things out with a positive example of self-shaming from a software company.

Here, the photo app subtly cops to the limitations of its facial recognition feature — probably not trained on dogs, to be fair — and gives you the option to make it try again with a pithy bit of self-mockery as CTA.

Well-played, folks. Well-played.
