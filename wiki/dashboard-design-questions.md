# 15 Questions for Designing Effective Dashboards

Effective dashboard design requires prioritizing relevant information, considering usability, and being guided by business goals. Here are 15 essential questions to ask at three distinct phases: understanding user goals, defining dashboard architecture, and executing visual design.

## Key Patterns & Concepts

- **Goal-Centric Design**: Identify specific problems solved, required information, and steps needed to complete tasks
- **Information Hierarchy**: Apply inverted pyramid method—most significant at top, details at bottom
- **Scanability**: Ensure users find information in 5 seconds; show trends and alerts; fit on single screen
- **Visual Hierarchy**: Use color, shape, labels to establish visual weight; simplify rather than over-decorate
- **Accessibility**: Use precise, legible, descriptive labels; choose best visualizations; maintain consistent type and colors
- **Responsive Design**: Move left-to-right, use proper spacing, incorporate white space for balance and clarity
- **Data Visualization Purpose**: Charts serve comparison, contrast, and analysis—not decoration

## Full Article

A dashboard is a single screen providing users with quick access to frequently requested data, KPIs, metrics, and other relevant analytics. Layout should present data in format easy to scan, with most important information immediately visible.

### Phase 1: User Goals

Designers should focus on identifying specific problems the dashboard solves, information required to achieve user goals, and number of steps users need to complete tasks. Design should be goal-centric and based on real problems.

Key questions:
- What specific problem is this design going to solve for the user?
- What information does the user need to successfully achieve their goal?
- What items are on the users' "To Do" list?
- How easily can the user start important tasks? How many steps do users need to take to achieve a specific goal?

### Phase 2: Dashboard Architecture

Create clear, intuitive dashboards showing actionable and useful information at a glance while containing vast amounts of information. Consider using inverted pyramid method to prioritize most significant information at top, followed by important details and trends in middle, and additional details at bottom.

Key questions:
- Is information clearly and efficiently organized for quick communication? Can my user find the information in 5 seconds?
- Does it implement visual hierarchy and data prioritization?
- What information is most actionable to one user type vs. another type?
- Does the dashboard show trends and changes in data over time?
- Does it show recent activity and any problems users should be aware of?
- Can this be arranged on a single screen so the information can be monitored at a glance?

### Phase 3: Visual Design

Prioritize simple visual design usually moving from left to right side of the screen. Use colors, shapes, and labels supporting overall structure. Ensure consistency in colors, typography, and graphic elements. Incorporate empty space for balance and clarity. Double margin space for main elements; provide white space between sections for better understanding.

Key questions:
- Am I using precise, legible and descriptive labels?
- Am I choosing the best data visualization graph or chart for the information?
- Does the dashboard use consistent type and colors?
- Is my iconography suggestive and easy to understand?
- Am I adding too many extra refinements? Overdoing shadows or textures? Using unnecessary images?
