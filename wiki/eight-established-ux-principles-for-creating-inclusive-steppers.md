# Eight Established Ux Principles For Creating Inclusive Steppers

Designing a stepper can be a complex process for UX designers. A stepper is essentially a multi-step process that guides users in completing a task or achieving a goal by breaking it down into smaller, more manageable steps. However, creating a stepper that is both user-friendly and accessible to all can pose challenges. In this article, we will explore best UX practices for designing an accessible and intuitive stepper with the help of examples.

## 1. Keep Steps Clear and Concise

The first rule to remember when designing a stepper is to keep the steps clear and straightforward. The language used should be easy to understand, especially for users with cognitive disabilities who may struggle with complex language.

**Example:** Instead of using jargon or technical terms, a simple e-commerce checkout process should use step labels like “Shipping,” “Payment,” and “Review” to ensure clarity.

## 2. Provide a Clear Progress Indicator

Users should always know their position in the stepper process. A clear indication of progress helps users understand how many steps they’ve completed and how many remain. This can be achieved through a progress bar that shows the completed and remaining steps.

**Example:** In a multi-step online survey, a progress bar at the top of the page might display “Step 2 of 5” to show users where they are.

## 3. Clear and Concise Instructions

Each step in the stepper should come with easy-to-understand instructions. This is particularly important for users with visual impairments who rely on screen readers to navigate the process. Instructions should be written in a way that can be easily interpreted by screen readers.

**Example:** When filling out an online job application, provide instructions such as “Please enter your contact information” to guide users effectively.

## 4. Use Clear and Visible Buttons

Buttons should be easy to see and understand. They should have clear labels and be positioned prominently for easy identification. It should be evident what each button does without any guesswork. Additionally, selected or active buttons should have a clear visual indication.

**Example:** In a sign-up process, use buttons with labels like “Next,” “Back,” and “Submit,” and make sure they are visibly distinct.

## 5. Provide Feedback

Feedback is crucial at every step of the stepper. Users should receive confirmation messages or visual indicators when a step is completed. This not only helps users understand their progress but also gives them a sense of accomplishment.

**Example:** After successfully submitting a job application, display a message like “Application Submitted” to provide feedback.

## 6. Consider Keyboard Navigation

Keyboard navigation is vital, especially for users with physical disabilities who may struggle with a mouse. Designers should ensure that users can easily navigate the stepper using only the keyboard, typically by using the tab key to move between buttons and fields.

**Example:** A user should be able to tab through each form field and button in a multi-step registration process without encountering navigation issues.

## 7. Use Clear and Visible Error Messages

Errors are a part of any form-filling process. Designers must ensure that error messages are not only clear but also highly visible. Users should be able to understand what went wrong and how to rectify it. The messages should be easy to locate and use clear, concise language.

**Example:** If a user forgets to complete a required field in an address form, display a message like “Please fill in the required fields.”

## 8. Test with Real Users

Finally, designers should rigorously test the stepper with actual users, especially those with disabilities. This ensures that the stepper is accessible to everyone and helps identify any usability issues that might be overlooked in the design process.

**Example:** Conduct usability tests with participants who rely on screen readers or keyboard navigation to verify the stepper’s accessibility.

In conclusion, designing an accessible and intuitive stepper may present challenges, but by adhering to these best UX practices, designers can create a user-friendly experience that accommodates all users. Clear language, visible buttons, feedback mechanisms, and keyboard navigation are all vital considerations in stepper design. Real-user testing ensures that the stepper is genuinely accessible to everyone, making it a valuable part of the design process.
